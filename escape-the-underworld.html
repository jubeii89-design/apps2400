    Escape the Underworld

    A 16-session D&D 5e campaign for six players. Hunt the relics of dead gods, die, and claw your way back out.

    DM only. This module contains spoilers: riddle answers, true names, and the full bestiary. Appendix D holds every answer key.

    Campaign Overview

    Logline: Six heroes hunting the relics of dead gods are murdered and buried by the one man those relics can kill. They must rise through the afterlife from nothing, and claw out of their own graves to finish the job.

    Sixteen 3-hour sessions (7–10pm, every two weeks), six players. The party starts alive at level 6 in the river town of Brindlewick, a rumor hub with no relics in it: the relics lie days away in Bloodfang Forest, the Old Ruins of Vael Iskar and the Hammerdeep Mines, and the players choose where to go. In Session 5 they are killed and buried. They begin the afterlife as level-1 heroes and climb through the three acts of the underworld. They climb out of their graves as the Returned at level 10 for a two-session finale. Fights aren't balanced: the DM rolls a d12 for encounters, some results are deadly, and the players decide when to fight, talk or run.

    Premise. A thousand years ago the six Old Gods died, and their relics scattered. Count Oswin Harrowmere, the Deathless Count, drank their stolen Last Breath: nothing can kill him unless all six relics are united as the Regalia of the Old Gods. Brother Caspel Vey, the last priest of the Old Gods, sends the party to find them.

    The turn. By Session 5 the party holds three relics and three of them are Mythic Heroes. Harrowmere comes for the relics, can't be killed, and kills them all. The town buries them. But a god's relic is bound to the soul that carries it, so the relics go with them into the underworld. Harrowmere sends his hunters after them there. To get home they need all six relics, and the Threshold Gate opens only for the Regalia.

    DM Verdict

    The structure is strong: a living world players love, a death that's a door, three acts that follow the blueprint, and a return that pays everything off. Two things make or break it:

    1. The level-1 reset has to feel like rebirth, not punishment. Hand out the new sheets in Session 6 as part of the fiction (they are souls, not bodies), and give the three Mythic Heroes their dimmed relic power from the first minute.
    2. The d12 tables do the unfair part; the fiction must telegraph it. Describe a Deadly roll so players can read it (the ground shakes, the river goes quiet) before initiative. Deaths then come from choices, which is the game.

    Core Systems

    Alive, the game runs on one rule: quests unlock only through the right question. Dead, it runs on four: the Tether, Soul Currency, the Relic Ladder and failure costs. Everything in the underworld is paid for in Memory Cards, Tether beads or allies, never in gold.

    The Right Question (Sessions 1–5)

    Nobody in Brindlewick hands out quests. Every town quest has a Key Question. An NPC reveals the quest only when a player asks something close to it, or beats a DC 18 Insight or Investigation check after a real conversation. Rumours point to questions, never to answers. Appendix A lists the 12 town quests and their Key Questions.

    Memory Cards

    At Session 0 each player writes five index cards, each a specific memory: a name, a place, a promise, a smell, a person they love. Cards are the underworld's money, the fuel for Mnemonic Arms and the thing monsters steal. When a card is lost, the player reads it aloud one last time and hands it to the DM. The character forgets it for good; it stays lost after the Return.

    Death, Burial and the Level-1 Reset

    After the massacre in Session 5, the town buries all six in Brindlewick's graveyard. Brother Caspel says the rites, Mayor Oake watches, and their gear is taken as grave goods. That is why they wake stripped of worldly items.

    In the underworld each player builds a new level-1 sheet of the same character, keeping name, backstory, Memory Cards and relic bond. They level once per session: 1 (S6), 2 (S7), 3 (S8–S9), 4 (S10), 5 (S11–S12), 6 (S13–S14), matching the blueprint's 1–3, 3–5 and 5–6.

    Hollow traits: resistance to necrotic damage; vulnerability to radiant damage; no need to eat, drink, sleep or breathe; undead wards don't stop them. Mortal healing heals half; Grave-Wine and Ash-Thread Bandages heal in full.

    Dying again: a PC dropped to 0 in the underworld dissolves and re-forms at the party's next long rest. The price is one Memory Card. If the whole party falls, they re-form on the Grey Docks, and the price is an ally.

    The Tether

    The blueprint's Mortal Essence becomes a shared party cord, built for six players.

    • Props: one red cord threaded with 12 beads, in the middle of the table.
    • Each long rest: the DM pulls 4 beads off the cord.
    • Protect each other's memories: a player reads one Memory Card aloud; another player becomes its Keeper by repeating it back. Each Kept card puts 1 bead back, up to 3 per rest. It takes about three minutes and builds the party's bonds every time.
    • Planar springs: drinking from a Mnemosyne Spring adds 3 beads; a Mnemosyne Vial adds 2. The cord holds 12 at most.
    • Monsters: "drain Essence" means pull 1 bead.
    • Fraying: if the cord would fall below 4 beads, pay each missing bead with a Memory Card or an ally.

    Soul Currency

    The dead don't take gold. One unit buys a small favour (a door, a rumour, a look the other way); three buy passage, a forged stamp or a guard who sleeps through his shift.

    • Secrets: a true secret about yourself or an ally. 1 unit.
    • Spells: surrender a spell slot until your next long rest (1 unit per slot level), or a known spell until the end of the act (3 units).
    • Memory Cards: 2 units; forgotten forever.
    • Tether beads: 1 bead = 1 unit.

    Failure Costs

    Failures in the underworld cost only two things, so they're easy to run.

    • A Memory Card: see above.
    • An ally: the named NPC is captured, killed or turned away, and their perk is gone for the rest of the campaign.

    Mythic Heroes and the Relic Ladder

    Each relic bonds to the first PC who touches it and makes them that Old God's Mythic Hero: six relics, six players. Three bond alive (S2, S3, S4), three dead (S8, S11, S13). The three carried at death arrive dimmed, and their power climbs with the hero:

    Level The relic grants
    1–2 Half-strength Mythic Trait; 1 mythic point; Mythic Step only
    3–4 Full Mythic Trait; 2 points; adds Mythic Strike
    5 3 points; adds Mythic Defiance; Mythic Surge unlocks
    6 Awakened: the Surge grows. The Gate needs six Awakened relics

    Mythic actions (once per round, at the end of another creature's turn): Mythic Step (1 point, move half speed without provoking opportunity attacks), Mythic Strike (1 point, one weapon attack or cantrip), Mythic Defiance (2 points, reroll a failed save). A relic bonded in the underworld starts at its holder's tier, and the new hero gets one free Surge that session. Each relic's trait and Surge are in Appendix C.

    The Relic Trials

    Every relic has a mini-boss the party must beat, no exceptions. The guardian can still be talked down, tricked or solved: the Riddlekeeper's riddles, Bromm's oath, honey for Keres-Tri. But when a hero lifts the relic, the Old God's trial-spirit tears loose. The relic won't bond until that spirit is defeated in combat.

    • No way around it. Trial-spirits can't be talked, bribed or tricked out of the fight. That's the god testing its new champion.
    • The Chosen. The PC who lifted the relic is the Chosen; each trial gives them a unique role in the fight.
    • Trial Weakness. Every spirit has a weakness tied to its god's theme. Players who think like the god win faster.
    • No death. At 0 hit points the spirit kneels and pours into the relic. That's the moment of apotheosis, and the Chosen becomes a Mythic Hero.
    • Pacing. The trial follows the guardian straight away, with no rest between. Budget 30–40 minutes of the session for it.

    Mnemonic Arms

    At the Spirit Forge (S7) each PC spends a Memory Card to forge a weapon, focus, shield or instrument from it: +0 at forging, +1 at level 4, +2 and a signature power at level 6. They stay Exalted after the Return.

    The Returned

    When the Gate opens (S14), each PC's Heartstone restores their body in the grave, and they claw their way out of Brindlewick's graveyard seven months after the massacre. They return at level 10 (their level 7 in life, plus one for each act survived) with:

    • +2 to one ability score (maximum 22); resistance to necrotic damage.
    • Once per long rest, drop to 1 hit point instead of 0.
    • See invisible and ethereal creatures within 30 ft.; advantage on saves against being frightened.
    • 4 mythic points, all six relics Awakened, and each Surge usable twice per long rest.
    • Every Memory Card and ally lost on the way stays lost.

    Factions

    • Harrowmere's Court (mortal world): Gravewardens, Bone Couriers, Lady Corvina, and Mayor Oake's bought town watch.
    • The Wardens: Veshanna's hunters, Reaper Acolytes, Reaper-Knights and Grave Hounds, under the Pale Marshal.

    Part One: Brindlewick (Sessions 0–5)

    Brindlewick is only a rumor hub: no relic lies within its walls, and every rumor points outward to one of three expeditions several days away. The party chooses the order of Bloodfang Forest, the Old Ruins of Vael Iskar and the Hammerdeep Mines; they come home as three Mythic Heroes to a town Mayor Oake has already sold, and Harrowmere kills them all.

    Session 0 — Character Creation & Consent

    • 6 PCs at level 6, alive, built as seasoned adventurers with a personal reason to hate the dead (or to fear them). Each PC gets one tie to Brindlewick or to Brother Caspel Vey.
    • Memory Cards: each player writes 5 memories (one line each; at least one painful, one tender, one about another PC). Say only that "memories are currency in this world"; do not explain when or how they will be taken.
    • The Tether (prop): a red cord with 12 beads and a cup of water, placed at the table's centre. Teach it as the party's shared thread: a player reads a Memory Card aloud, another player becomes its Keeper and repeats it back, and the DM slides a bead back. In Part One it tracks dread on expeditions (pulled 2 beads per night in a place of dread; Kept cards restore 1 bead each, max 3 per rest); with fewer than 4 beads, the party has disadvantage on Wisdom saves until one is restored. Do not explain where the cord leads later.
    • The open-world rule: "Nobody in Brindlewick points you at a door. Rumors point at three places; you choose which to go to and in what order. The world does not wait politely, and the last place you visit will not be empty." Also teach that encounters are not balanced: talk, scout, retreat, use terrain.
    • Consent talk (lines and veils, X-card, safety tools) with one explicit item: "Character death as a story turn." Tell the table the campaign contains it, that each PC will choose their own final moment, and that the story continues after it. Do not reveal when or how. Ask for a yes from every player; note objections privately.
    • Why the party is in Brindlewick: each PC received a sealed letter from Brother Caspel Vey, last priest of the Old Gods, with a Brindlewick Ferry-Token and one sentence: "The Deathless Count can be ended, and you are the ones I trust to end him." They arrive on the same morning ferry from different corners of the river. Each PC takes one hook (an old debt to Caspel, a dream of a dead god, a family grave in the Brindlewick graveyard, a Vellmark grudge).
    • Close: each PC says, in one sentence, what they are carrying that they would hate to lose.

    Session 1 — Brindlewick (Level 6)

    Goal: Arrive, meet Brother Caspel Vey, accept the relic commission, hear at least two rumors for each of the three sites, and choose the first expedition.

    Opening shot: Crane down from the fog over the river bend as the morning ferry noses into Brindlewick's docks, chimneys smoking, bells muffled. Close on a PC's Brindlewick Ferry-Token pressed into the ferryman's palm.

    Timeline (7:00–10:00):

    • 7:00–7:20 Recap of S0; ferry arrival; Ferrymaster Hob Tarn (TQ5) and the market crowd. Place the Tether on the table.
    • 7:20–8:10 Market and the Gutted Eel, rumors only (TQ1, TQ2, TQ3, TQ4, TQ7, TQ9, TQ10, TQ12 seeded). NPCs hint and never answer; teach the Right Question with one failed ask and one successful ask. Use the Rumor Board below.
    • 8:10–8:55 The Drowned Chapel: Caspel's commission. The six relics, the Regalia, the Deathless Count. He names three relics still "in the world, in places where doors, hammers and dawn could not follow": Dawnshard, Riddlestone of Iskar, Thunderspike of Ardun.     • Optional: a drunk Harrowmere Gravewarden × 1 (Hard) at the Eel; disarm with Persuasion or Intimidation, or let the barkeep's bouncer end it.

    Expeditions (Sessions 2–4, any order)

    Run one expedition per session. Travel is abridged: roll 1 d12 per day of travel on that region's table (Bloodfang Forest d12, Vael Iskar Road d12, Hammerdeep Foothills d12), one roll out and one back if the players want it, and use the Tether for dread. The party is Level 6 for the first two expeditions and becomes Level 7 after the second. Lady Corvina Thrane reaches the LAST site first: she and her Gravewardens are already inside; the party must take the relic from her, bargain, or race her out. Each site has a "last-site" variant line in its Timeline.

    Expedition: Bloodfang Forest (≈5 days round trip; Level 6–7)

    Rumors that point here

    • TQ2 Ilse Corrow: her husband rafted timber past the forest and swore "the sun doesn't rise over it"; his charts mark a river landing two days out (true).
    • TQ9 Tam Bellwether: boatwrights refuse Bloodfang timber, "cursed by a dead witch's blood" (wrong: the wood bleeds because the dawn is gone, not from a witch).
    • TQ7 Old Marrin: the lullaby "Mother of the Long Night," whose last verse says she "sleeps when the light is held to her eyes" (half-true: the light must be the Dawnshard's, not any lamp).
    • Caspel, only if asked about Solenne: the dawn goddess's shard was "last seen going east, toward the wood nobody names" (true, but he hid that he always knew).

    The journey: River barge upstream, then two days on foot through a forest where the trunks are black and the leaves dark red. Landmark: the Feeding Post, a rusted iron stake ringed with old offerings (bowls, toys, a child's shoe), the last sign of the people who once made peace with the forest. Table: Bloodfang Forest d12. The dread: each night in the forest pulls 2 Tether beads (Day 1 and Day 2); a fire, a Grave-Candle or a spoken Kept memory restores one.

    The site

    1. The Teeth of the Wood. Two lines of pale, fang-shaped stumps mark a boundary. Bloodfang Wolf × 4 watch from the brush and do not attack those who leave offerings or show an open palm (Animal Handling DC 14); they fear fire. Avoid: walk the pilgrim path by the Feeding Post.
    2. The Weeping Road. A gully where the trees drip red sap; tracks converge on a spot where a Bloodfang Stalker × 2 hangs upside down in the canopy and drops onto the rearmost traveller. Stealth DC 16 in single file, or a sunlit clearing the Stalkers will not cross. A dead hunter's journal names the heart-tree.
    3. The Dryad's Grove. A ring of thorns around a pale oak. Thornbound Dryad × 2 bar the way, bound by roots that bleed for want of light. Talk: Persuasion DC 15 and a Grave-Candle lit and set at her feet; she remembers dawn, names the Mother, and offers a path to the heart. Or fight and the thorns close on those who draw steel.
    4. The Den of Bones. A sinkhole ribbed with antlers and human ribs. Bloodfang Stalker × 1 and Bloodfang Wolf × 3 guard three pups that are not corrupted (Insight DC 14 shows they are whole); harming them turns the Dryads hostile; leaving them food opens the tunnel to the root-cellar beneath the heart-tree.
    5. The Heart-Tree. A dead giant whose roots cup a pit of red mud; at its base a faint glow like a coal under ash. The Fanged Mother (see Boss) lies in it.

    Boss: The Fanged Mother. A wolf the size of a cart, white fur matted red, her eyes full of the night; her lair is the root-pit, where every step drips blood and every shadow is her pup. To win without killing: lift the Dawnshard from the roots (Athletics DC 14 through the roots) and hold it to her eyes at first light (or from a lit Grave-Candle, the shard shining brighter for it) and she stops, shrinks, and lies down, a great grey wolf. Fight her and the lair moves: roots grab, the pit floods.

    Relic Trial: The Unrisen Dawn (CR 8). When a PC lifts the shard, a burnt-out sun in a woman's shape steps out of the heart-tree and the forest goes black. Fight it here, straight after the Fanged Mother, with no rest. The Chosen must keep the shard held high while it tries to snuff the light. It can't be talked down. At 0 hit points it kneels and pours into the shard.

    Relic moment: The shard comes up out of the red mud warm as a living hand; the first PC to touch it feels dawn rise behind their ribs, and the whole forest, for one breath, turns gold. Dawnshard; Mythic Hero.

    Expedition: The Old Ruins of Vael Iskar (≈7 days round trip; Level 6–7)

    Rumors that point here

    The site (three levels down; each door is a talking door, and Doorwardens are the ruins' slowest guards)

    1. The Gate Court (surface). A collapsed courtyard with a pair of great bronze doors. Grinwold, the Gate, is pompous and lonely and adores being addressed correctly. Price: "Tell me a secret you have never told," told honestly (a true secret of a PC, or a PC's Memory Card read aloud; the door keeps it and never repeats it). A Doorwarden × 1 slams shut on those who skip it.
    2. The Scriptorium (Level 1). Rows of desks, silent scribes. Hollow Scribe × 2 write your names into the floor. Mabb and Mabbit, twin doors, argue: Mabb always lies and Mabbit always tells the truth, and they swap at the first lie told in the room. To pass: one question, and "What would the other say?" works once. Hurt a scribe and the doors seal.
    3. The Corridor of Seven Doors (Level 2). Seven doors, six false. Old Sill is a timid door who hates being opened and begs to be oiled. Price: a kindness (oil, a tale, a lantern left burning for it). He warns of the Ruin Crawler × 3 behind the false doors, who are drawn to noise. Wrong doors drop into a pit of crawlers.
    4. The Cistern of Mirrors (Level 3). A drowned cellar of black water and a mirrored door, the Hollow Door, which asks, "What are you most afraid of losing?"     5. The Vault of the Last Question (Level 3). A round room of keys hanging from the ceiling like rain. The Last Riddlekeeper waits beside the final door, an old priest whose body has become part of the frame.

    Boss: The Last Riddlekeeper. A robed figure grown into the vault door, a hundred keys in his hair, lair lit by the faint green glow of each key that remembers a question. He has guarded the Riddlestone for a thousand years and wants one thing: someone to ask him the question he cannot answer. To win without killing: ask "Who keeps the keeper?", then answer it aloud: "Whoever asks." He laughs, hands over the Riddlestone, and the door opens. Or solve his three riddles: one of his, one of yours, one neither knows (questions and answers in Appendix D). Fighting him brings every door in the ruins slamming shut.

    Relic Trial: The Unanswered (CR 8). When a PC lifts the stone, every key in the vault turns at once and a doorway of mouths opens in the air. It asks a question each round; the Chosen must answer truthfully, aloud. It can't be talked down, even though it only talks. At 0 hit points it closes and pours into the stone.

    Relic moment: The stone is a smooth river-pebble with an eye painted on it; the PC who lifts it hears a dozen doors, in every age, say "Yes" at once. Riddlestone of Iskar; Mythic Hero.

    Session 5 addendum — The Burial

    (Read aloud at the end of S5, or as the S6 cold open. Play it once, slowly.)

    Three days later the whole of Brindlewick stands in the graveyard in the rain. Brother Caspel Vey says the old rites over six plain boxes, his voice cracking on the names of gods nobody has prayed to in a thousand years; Mayor Tamsin Oake stands at the front with her hands folded and does not look at the graves, and Wren Tarn watches her from behind the yew. Under Oake's quiet eye the sexton takes the rings, the swords, the holy symbols and every coin "as grave goods for the crossing", and a Gravewarden in a black cloak carries the rest back to Vellmark in a sack. Nothing of the Thunderspike, the Dawnshard or the Riddlestone of Iskar is found on the bodies, and Harrowmere's men make a note of it.

    The shot from inside the grave: planks, dark, the weight of the lid; a slit of grey light, Oake's face leaning over it with a lantern and a handful of earth in her fist. She opens her fingers. The light narrows to a line, to a thread, to nothing, and the soil begins to thump. Somewhere very far below, ledger paper turns.

    Artifacts: the Six Relics

    Mythic Heroes and the Relic Ladder

    Bonding. When a PC first touches a relic, the relic chooses them and they become that Old God's Mythic Hero. Six relics, six PCs; a PC can bond to only one relic. Attuning to a relic does not count against the 3-item limit. Every relic is soul-bound and divine, not "worldly": when its bearer dies it follows their soul and is never stripped at the burial, which is how Thunderspike of Ardun, Dawnshard and Riddlestone of Iskar reach the Underworld after S5, dim. Three Mythic Heroes exist by S4 (S2, S3, S4), four by S8, five by S11, all six by S13.

    Mythic points and actions. Once per round, at the end of another creature's turn, a Mythic Hero may spend mythic points on a mythic action: Mythic Strike (1 point: make one weapon attack or cast one cantrip), Mythic Step (1 point: move up to half your speed without provoking opportunity attacks), or Mythic Defiance (2 points: reroll a failed saving throw and take the new result). Points return on a long rest. Each relic grants a Mythic Trait (always on), two minor properties (they wake at L3), and a Mythic Surge (once per long rest). In Part One (L6-7, before the Fall) a Hero has 3 points, all three actions and the base Surge.
